# Interlude "130 Years of Immunotherapy": reader-experience review

Reviewed: `content/drafts/interlude-history.md` (line numbers refer to that file). Reader persona: an intelligent non-biologist who has read Chapters 1-7. Checked against PLAN.md sections 1-3.

## Overall impression

The opening is the best hook in Part II/III so far (Bessie Dashiell, then Coley hunting through the tenements for Stein), and the Allison and Honjo section carries real human drama. The arc is mostly sound, but it is told more than shown. The "hype, then winter" middle is asserted in two sentences while the text itself lists a string of approvals through those years. Sections 2-3 re-teach Chapter 7 and then rush through a chronological parade of firsts and drug names. The timeline is a lovely idea, but at 35 milestones, 7 lanes, 5 marker shapes, 5 era bands, 2 modes and a pace strip it teaches several ideas at once, and it sits right after the first paragraph, before anyone can decode it. Several statements (the accelerator-versus-brakes thesis, "every lane lights up after 2010") are contradicted by the chapter's own content, and a sharp reader will notice. Fix those and trim the figure, and this becomes a strong bridge into Part III.

Strengths to keep: the Coley/Stein opening; Allison's blind repeat over Christmas and five years of pharma indifference; "The name misled" for PD-1; the honest "most patients still don't respond" ending; the numbered "five reasons"; the tremelimumab box's point that failure in one trial, at one dose, is not failure of the idea.

The closing "The timeline doubles as a map" refers to a figure the reader scrolled past roughly 3,000 words earlier.
- Fix: Move the whole `:::figure int-timeline` block to just after the "Many roads at once" list (after l.342), with a lead-in: "You have now met most of the cast. Here is the whole story at once. Tap any marker." Revise intro ¶3 (l.13) to: "That hunch is the seed of immunotherapy: turning the body's own defenses against cancer. It took about 120 years to bear fruit, through false dawns, abandoned theories and a few stubborn people. This chapter tells that story, then ends with a map of what Part III covers."

**2. The central thesis ("every approach pressed the accelerator") is contradicted by the chapter's own examples.**
- Location: l.304 "Every approach so far pressed the accelerator, with bacteria, cytokines or more T cells."; key-idea l.317 "For a century, immunotherapy tried to push the immune system harder."; lesson 1, l.362 "Every strategy pressed the accelerator."; takeaway l.410.
- Problem: The preceding section celebrates rituximab and trastuzumab (1997-98) and BCG. A skeptical reader asks "which is it? Antibodies were a huge success and they don't push anything." The antibodies are never placed in the accelerator/brake frame, so the thesis reads as tidier than the history.
- Fix: Scope the claim and place the antibodies explicitly. End l.292 with: "Antibody drugs like these were a different, more precise kind of success: they marked cancer cells for the immune system's attention but did not rouse its T cells." Then l.304: "Almost every attempt to rouse T cells so far pressed the accelerator, with bacteria, cytokines or more T cells." Key-idea: "For a century, the main way to rouse T cells was to push harder: more bacteria, more cytokines, more T cells. The breakthrough came from asking how the immune system is held back, and blocking the brakes that tumors exploit."

**3. The "long winter" is asserted, not shown, and the "hype" half of hope-hype-failure is missing.**
- Location: l.314 "Then came a long winter. In 2008 a 655-patient phase 3 trial..."; l.310 "After so many failed vaccine and cytokine trials"; l.374 "Every era had its great hope (Coley's toxins, interferon, IL-2)".
- Problem: Between 1976 and 2006 the reader reads only wins: BCG, interferon approved, IL-2 approved, TIL works, rituximab, trastuzumab, HPV vaccine. The first mention of "failed vaccine and cytokine trials" is a backstory aside at l.310. The closing line claims interferon and IL-2 were hyped great hopes that fell short, but the main text never depicted the hype or the fall. A reader asks "winter? It sounds like immunotherapy was succeeding."
- Fix: Add a short paragraph before l.314 that shows both halves, then trim the "Then came a long winter" line. Draft (all specifics need verification before use; suggested sources: press coverage of interferon around 1980 and of Rosenberg's LAK/IL-2 results around 1985; the Rosenberg 2004 *Nat Med* review of cancer-vaccine response rates): "Hope ran ahead of the evidence. Interferon was splashed across magazine covers as a possible cure; it helped a few rare cancers and little else. Through the 1990s and early 2000s, experimental cancer vaccines were tried in hundreds of patients and tumors shrank in only a small fraction, [N] percent in one center's own tally. Immunotherapy earned a reputation as a field that cured mice and failed people. That was the climate Allison walked into." Also reconcile era-band names with this paragraph (see item 8).

**4. Trial vocabulary is never defined, and Chapter 8 (which defines it) comes after this chapter.**
- Location: l.314 "phase 3 trial"; l.322 "phase 3 trial ... Median survival rose from 6.4 to 10 months"; l.337 "12-month event-free survival"; l.338 "The hazard ratio was 0.56, a statistically borderline result" and "a futility boundary"; card `interpath-2026` ("primary endpoint", "hazard ratio of 0.56"); no earlier chapter defines "phase 3", "randomized trial", "median" or "response". Chapter 8 defines hazard ratio and response rate later.
- Problem: The chapter's drama rests on trial results a lay reader cannot parse. "Hazard ratio of 0.56, statistically borderline" and "crossing a futility boundary" are opaque. The two Part III-adjacent terms the reader most needs ("median survival", "response") are glossed nowhere in this chapter.
- Fix: (a) Add a short clinic box before "The dam breaks": "Three phrases recur below. A **phase 3 trial** is the large, final test, usually comparing a new treatment with the standard one in hundreds of patients assigned by chance. **Median survival** is the time by which half of the patients have died; a gain from 6.4 to 10 months moves that midpoint but says nothing about the few who live far longer. A **response** means tumors shrank by a set amount; it is not the same as cure." (b) Rewrite l.338 bullet: "...reduced melanoma recurrence in a 157-patient trial: the risk of recurrence or death was 44% lower than with pembrolizumab alone, though the trial was small and the result was statistically borderline." (c) Replace "crossing a futility boundary" with "was stopped early after an interim look showed it was unlikely to succeed." (d) Add glossary entries for phase 3 trial, median survival, event-free survival, response.

**5. "Does the body police cancer?" re-teaches Chapter 7, nearly verbatim, including the Go-deeper box.**
- Location: l.276-288. Chapter 7 (l.21-36 of `07-escape.md`) already tells Burnet and Thomas, the 1974 nude mice, the 2001 Schreiber revival and why the 1974 experiment misled a generation (thymus-less mice still have some T cells and NK cells; the CBA/H enzyme point). The interlude also repeats it in two timeline cards.
- Problem: A reader who just finished Chapter 7 reads the same story again, with the same lesson, and the box adds nothing new. About 450 words are spent on repetition, while the winter (item 3) and the tremelimumab twin (item 6) get a sentence each.
- Fix: Reduce to a short bridge and delete the box: "Coley's work raised a deeper question: does the immune system notice cancer at all? Paul Ehrlich guessed yes in 1909. In the late 1950s Macfarlane Burnet and Lewis Thomas turned the guess into a hypothesis, immunosurveillance (Chapter 7). In 1974 a mouse experiment seemed to refute it, and for about 25 years most researchers, and most of those who funded drug development, treated tumor immunity as a dead end. Chapter 7 shows how better mice revived the idea. What matters here is the cost: a field whose basic premise is doubted struggles to win money and recruits." Keep the two timeline cards but shorten them (item 12).

**6. The most dramatic tension in the story, two sibling drugs with opposite verdicts, is buried in a skippable box; lesson 2 uses a term never defined in the main text.**
- Location: l.314 and l.322 (main text); l.368-372 (box "When a failed trial isn't the end"); l.363 "LAK cells worked in mice only against tumors too young to have their own blood supply, and they did nothing for their first 30 patients."
- Problems: (a) After "the 2008 trial of another anti-CTLA-4 antibody failed", the reader reads "ipilimumab succeeded" and asks the obvious question, "same target, why did one fail?" The answer sits in a collapsed box. (b) Lesson 4 ("Trials built around averages can miss that") has no example in the main text. (c) "LAK cells" first appear in the Portraits box and in lesson 2, so a main-text-only reader meets an undefined acronym. (d) "They did nothing for their first 30 patients" conflicts with the Portraits box ("IL-2 alone did the work and the cells added nothing"), and "tumors too young to have their own blood supply" needs an angiogenesis idea the reader doesn't have.
- Fix: Rewrite l.314: "Then came a long winter. In 2008 a 655-patient trial of tremelimumab, a sister of Allison's antibody, was stopped because it did not beat chemotherapy in melanoma. But the trial held a clue: responses were no more frequent than with chemotherapy, yet they lasted much longer. An average hides that kind of difference. At the time it looked like one more immunotherapy that cured mice and failed people." Keep the box for the numbers. Rewrite lesson 2: "**Animal models misled.** Nude mice wrongly 'disproved' immunosurveillance. Lymphokine-activated killer (LAK) cells, immune cells stimulated in a dish, looked promising in mice but added nothing beyond IL-2 alone in people."

**7. The figure's data contradict its own punchline, and the lane scheme overlaps and doesn't match Part III.**
- Location: spec: "every lane lighting up after 2010"; `alt`: "After 2010 every lane fills rapidly with approvals"; data lanes.

That is five lanes instead of seven, each with a chapter. If you keep cytokines separate, say so in a caption ("The cytokine lane goes quiet after 1992 because high-dose IL-2 is rarely used today") and drop the false claim. Change the alt text to "After 2010 most lanes fill rapidly..."

**8. The figure teaches too many ideas; cut it to one.**
