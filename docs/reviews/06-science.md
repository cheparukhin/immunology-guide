# Science review: Chapter 6, "When Cells Go Rogue" (`content/drafts/06-cancer.md`)

Reviewer: scientific review (cancer genomics, clonal evolution, tumor antigens, neoantigens, TMB/MSI). Line numbers refer to the draft as of 2026-10-05. Knowledge cutoff for status claims: October 2026. Sources were checked against PubMed/PMC primary texts, FDA records and the Amass regulatory core; PMIDs and URLs are given per item.

## Overall assessment

This is the strongest draft I have reviewed in this series on raw factual accuracy: all 35 sources exist, every author/journal/year/volume/page/DOI matches, every `[^n]` marker (1–35) is used at least once, and essentially every number in the main narrative checks out against the primary text — the eyelid-skin figures (234 biopsies, four donors aged 55–73, ~140 driver mutations/cm², "over a quarter of cells"), two-to-eight drivers and 20–30 years, the 10× smoker/never-smoker ratio, 2.2 million infection-attributable cancers, 8% germline carriers, Knudson's 48 cases and "average of three" tumors, Yachida's decade-plus-five-years, TRACERx's 327 regions and ">75%" subclonal drivers, the three Hanahan papers, NY-ESO-1 in 20/25 synovial sarcomas with 6 heterogeneous, MAGE-A1/A4 at 20%/9% versus 51%/44%, vitiligo in 3.4% of 5,737, the whole Tran 2016 KRAS G12D case down to 1.11×10¹¹ cells and the chromosome-6 loss, Parkhurst's 62/75 and 1.6% and 99%, TESLA's 37/608, Le 2017's "24 of 32 tumor subtypes" and ">5%" organs and the >100-fold MANA2 anchor result, Turajlic's ninefold indel enrichment, Yarchoan's 27 types and 55%, and Chalmers' 62,150 samples with 97%/16%. The `ch06-tmb` data are also sound: the computed medians sit within a factor of ~1.2 of independently published values (melanoma 14.9 vs Chalmers 13.5; lung squamous 7.9 vs 9.0; lung adenocarcinoma 6.7 vs 6.3; Vogelstein's ~200 nonsynonymous mutations per melanoma/lung tumor ≈ 6.7/Mb), and the MSS↔MSI-H ~11× gap, the uveal/skin melanoma contrast and the POLE tail are all real. The oncogene/tumor-suppressor–versus–immune-brake confusion is handled better than in most textbooks (the `key-idea` at line 71 is excellent and should be preserved verbatim). What needs work is concentrated in the figure specs: one HLA allele choice contradicts the published literature and the spec's own rule, the KRAS DNA strip is too short to let a reader trace the peptide, the clonal-evolution tuning target contradicts the chapter's own eyelid number, and the TMB chart's source line misdescribes the pediatric cohort. One prose number — Cancer Research UK's preventable fraction — is out of date. Beyond that it is calibration: viral antigens are oversold slightly, cancer-testis antigens are described too absolutely, and 35 sources should come down to ~27.

## Must fix

**M1. `ch06-typo-to-target`: Patient B carries HLA-A\*02:01, the one remaining allele with published mutant-KRAS presentation (lines 430–436; caption line 487)**
- Quote: "Patient B cups: A*02, A*24, B*44, B*51, C*05, C*07. (Both patients are hypothetical. Do not put HLA-A*03 or HLA-A*11 in either patient: those types are reported to display some mutant-KRAS fragments, which would muddy the point.)" and step 4's caption: "Switch to Patient B: none of their six grooves can hold it, and the typo stays invisible."
- Problem: the spec's own rule is right but incomplete. Peptides spanning codon 12 mutants of RAS — explicitly including G12D — have been reported to bind **HLA-A\*02:01** as well as HLA-A\*03:01: Baleeiro RB, et al. "Optimized Anchor-Modified Peptides Targeting Mutated RAS Are Promising Candidates for Immunotherapy," *Front Immunol* 2022;13:902709, PMID 35720289, doi:10.3389/fimmu.2022.902709 — "We have identified immunogenic peptides derived from codon 12 mutant RAS (G12A, G12C, G12D, G12R, G12S and G12V), which bind to HLA-A\*02:01 and HLA-A\*03:01 and elicit strong peptide-specific CD8+ T cell responses" (anchor modification then *enhanced* that binding; the native peptides already bind). A\*02:01-restricted anti-KRAS TCR-mimic CAR and bispecific programs are also published (Ebrahimi S, et al. *J Mol Med* 2025, PMID 40794198, doi:10.1007/s00109-025-02585-2). A\*02:01 is also the most-studied allele in the world, so it is the one an expert reader will check first. Putting it in the "cannot display it" patient makes the figure's single most important assertion contestable.
- Correct, well-evidenced presenters of mutant KRAS G12D: **HLA-C\*08:02** (clinically validated: Tran 2016, PMID 27959684; structures and TCR affinities in Sim et al., *PNAS* 2020, PMID 32461371 — "Four T cell receptors… recognized two KRAS-G12D neoantigens, a nonamer and a decamer, all restricted by HLA-C\*08:02"; TCR gene therapy in pancreatic cancer, Leidner et al., *NEJM* 2022, PMID 35648703) and **HLA-A\*11:01** (affinity-enhanced TCR against the G12D 10-mer, *Nat Commun* 2022, PMID 36088370; crystal structure PDB 7OW4).
- Fix: swap A\*02 out of Patient B and widen the spec's ban. Suggested Patient B cups: **A\*29:02, A\*24:02, B\*44:03, B\*51:01, C\*07:01, C\*12:03**. Extend the builder note to: "Do not put HLA-A\*02, A\*03 or A\*11 in Patient B: all three have published reports of binding codon-12 mutant RAS peptides." Soften the caption to: "Switch to Patient B: none of their six grooves holds this fragment well enough to display it, and the typo stays invisible." (Optionally cite the population-scale justification: Wells-style analysis in *Front Immunol* 2020, PMID 32256484 — "common cancer mutations including BRAF-V600E and KRAS-G12D are predicted to bind none of the common HLA alleles, and are thus 'immunogenically silent' in the human population.")

**M2. `ch06-typo-to-target`: the DNA strip stops three codons short of the peptide the figure then displays (lines 419–421 vs 426–427)**
- Quote: "a strip of DNA letters from the start of the KRAS gene, codons spaced in triplets: ATG ACT GAA TAT AAA CTT GTG GTA GTT GGA GCT GGT GGC GTA GGC." Then step 3: "one fragment stays, shown as 9 beads with letters G A D G V G K S A".
- Problem: those 15 codons encode residues 1–15 (M T E Y K L V V V G A G G V G). The displayed 9-mer GADGVGKSA is residues **10–18**, so codons 16–18 (K, S, A) are missing from the strip. The figure's whole promise is a traceable chain DNA → protein → peptide; as specified, a reader who tries to trace the fragment back to the letters runs off the end of the strip. (The protein step at line 422 correctly shows residues 1–18.)
- Correct sequence: KRAS codons 16–18 are **AAG AGT GCC** (K, S, A) — reference coding sequence NM_033360: …GGA GCT GGT GGC GTA GGC AAG AGT GCC…
- Fix: extend the strip to 18 codons: "ATG ACT GAA TAT AAA CTT GTG GTA GTT GGA GCT GGT GGC GTA GGC AAG AGT GCC", and in step 3 underline codons 10–18 as the stretch the fragment comes from. (Everything else here is right: codon 12 is GGT, G→A in the middle position gives GAT = aspartic acid, and GADGVGKSA / GADGVGKSAL are exactly the two epitopes in Tran 2016.)

**M3. "Cancer Research UK's estimate that 42% of those cancers are preventable" is out of date (line 43)**
- Quote: "Their numbers sit comfortably with Cancer Research UK's estimate that 42% of those cancers are preventable."
- Problem: 42% is the figure Tomasetti, Li & Vogelstein quoted in 2017 ("Cancer Research UK estimates that 42% of these cancer cases are preventable" — PMC5852673), but it predates CRUK's current estimate. CRUK now states **37.7% (about 4 in 10)** of UK cancer cases are preventable, from Brown KF, Rumgay H, Dunlop C, et al. "The fraction of cancer attributable to modifiable risk factors in England, Wales, Scotland, Northern Ireland, and the United Kingdom in 2015." *Br J Cancer* 2018 (PMID 29567982, doi:10.1038/s41416-018-0029-6; CRUK's own page: https://www.cancerresearchuk.org/health-professional/cancer-statistics/statistics-by-cancer-type/all-cancers-combined/risk/preventable-cancers). Attributing a wrong number to a named organization is exactly the kind of thing a reader will check.
- Rewrite: "Their numbers sit comfortably with Cancer Research UK's estimate that around 4 in 10 UK cancers — 38% — are preventable." (Add Brown 2018 as a source, or drop the attribution and write "with epidemiological estimates that roughly 40% of cancers are preventable".)

**M4. `ch06-clonal-evolution`: step 3's tuning target contradicts the chapter's own eyelid number (line 162–163)**
- Quote: "The 'Cells with ≥1 driver' counter climbs to ~10–25% (callback to the eyelid study)."
- Problem: the chapter opens with "Roughly a quarter of the cells carried a mutation in a known cancer gene" (line 11), and Martincorena 2015 reports "Positively selected mutations were found in **18 to 32%** of normal skin cells… over a quarter of cells carrying cancer-causing mutations" (PMID 25999502). A simulation tuned to land at 10–25% will routinely display ~12–15% at the exact moment the caption invites the reader to recall "just like the eyelid skin", undercutting the callback with the chapter's own cited figure.
- Fix: change the target to "**~20–30%**", and add to the tuning targets: "At the end of step 3 the counter should read between 20% and 30%, the range measured in normal eyelid skin (18–32%)."

**M5. `ch06-tmb`: the on-chart source line misdescribes the pediatric cohort, and the derived medians are not reconciled with the published ones (lines 543–545, 598–599, 630; prose line 515)**
- Quote (source line): "Childhood cancers: Gröbner et al., Nature 2018 (795 primary tumors), via cBioPortal." Prose: "nearly 800 childhood tumors from a German-led study."
- Problems: (a) the published cohort is **961 tumors from 24 molecular types in children, adolescents and young adults**, of which **879 were primary tumors** used for the mutation-frequency analysis (Gröbner et al., *Nature* 2018;555:321–327, PMID 29489754; abstract: "a pan-cancer cohort including 961 tumours from children, adolescents, and young adults, comprising 24 distinct molecular types"; mutation-frequency analysis: "24 pediatric (n = 879 primary tumours)"). 795 is a cBioPortal-derived subset, not the study's n, and presenting it as the study's n is a sourcing error on a chart whose stated virtue is honesty. (b) "childhood" silently drops the adolescents and young adults. (c) The same paper reports a median of **0.13 mutations/Mb** for the pediatric cohort (vs 1.8/Mb for TCGA adults), while the chart's childhood medians run 0.10–0.33 with an overall 0.23 — i.e. the chart's values are ~1.5–2× the published median for the same patients, almost certainly because of a different exome denominator. Unreconciled, that invites a reviewer to conclude the numbers are wrong.
- Fix: change the source line to: "Childhood, adolescent and young-adult cancers: Gröbner et al., *Nature* 2018 (961 tumors, 24 types; 795 primary tumors with mutation calls in cBioPortal). Adult cancers: TCGA PanCancer Atlas (about 10,000 tumors, whole-exome sequencing), via cBioPortal. Each dot = 5% of tumors of that type. Counts depend on method: the published median for this pediatric cohort is 0.13 mutations per megabase against 1.8 for TCGA adults; clinical tests report different absolute numbers again." Change the prose at line 515 to "nearly 800 tumors from children and young adults in a German-led study." Keep the 150-fold headline — it is conservative under either denominator.

## Should fix

**S1. Tomasetti 2017's 66% is a share of *driver gene* mutations, not of all mutations (line 43)**
- Quote: "Across 32 cancer types in the United Kingdom, they calculated that 66% of the *mutations* in cancers arose from copying errors, 29% from the environment and 5% from inheritance."
- Problem: the paper's estimate is explicitly for driver gene mutations — "The median proportion of **driver gene mutations** attributable to E was 23% among all cancer types… we calculate that 29% of the mutations in cancers occurring in the United Kingdom were attributable to E, 5%… to H, and 66%… to R" (PMC5852673, Fig. 4). The chapter's very next sentence reasons about driver hits, so the precise reading is also the clearer one.
- Rewrite: "…they calculated that 66% of the *driver* mutations in those cancers arose from copying errors, 29% from the environment and 5% from inheritance."

**S2. Citation mismatch: metastasis mortality is sourced to a pancreatic-timing paper (line 21)**
- Quote: "This is {{metastasis|metastasis}}, the most common cause of death from cancer.[^2]"
- Problem: [^2] is Yachida 2010, which is about the timing of metastasis in pancreatic cancer and says nothing about the share of cancer deaths. (It *does* correctly support the two later uses at lines 78 and 80.)
- Fix: either drop the marker, or cite Dillekås H, Rogers MS, Straume O. "Are 90% of deaths from cancer caused by metastases?" *Cancer Med* 2019;8:5574–5576 (PMID 31397113, doi:10.1002/cam4.2474), which supports "most" with real data while showing the familiar "90%" is weakly evidenced: in Norwegian registry data "for solid tumors, 66.7% of cancer deaths were registered with metastases as a contributing cause." Keep the wording "the most common cause of death from cancer" rather than quoting a percentage.

**S3. Citation mismatch: mutational signatures are sourced to Lawrence 2013 instead of Alexandrov 2013 (lines 31–32)**
- Quote: "imperfect repairs leave a characteristic pattern of letter swaps written all over the genomes of skin melanomas.[^4]" and "Its chemicals damage DNA in the airways with a pattern of their own.[^4]"
- Problem: [^4] (Lawrence 2013, PMID 23770567) is about mutational heterogeneity and false-positive driver calls — its abstract never discusses UV or tobacco signatures. The signature claims belong to [^8] (Alexandrov 2013, PMID 23945592: "we analysed 4,938,362 mutations from 7,042 cancers and extracted more than 20 distinct mutational signatures… associated with age of the patient at cancer diagnosis, known mutagenic exposures or defects in DNA maintenance"), which the chapter already cites at line 36.
- Fix: change both markers to [^8]. Keep [^4] where it belongs — the thousandfold TMB range at line 509 (Lawrence: "the median frequency of non-synonymous mutations varied by more than 1000-fold across cancer types", pediatric ~0.1/Mb to melanoma and lung >100/Mb).

**S4. Citation mismatch: the melanoma half of the tumor-associated antigen sentence is sourced to a HER2 review (line 234)**
- Quote: "…like HER2, overexpressed in about 15–20% of breast cancers, or that it inherits from its tissue of origin, like the pigment-making proteins of melanoma.[^22]"
- Problem: [^22] (Loibl & Gianni, *Lancet* 2017) covers the HER2 clause only; it is not a source for melanocyte differentiation antigens.
- Fix: "…like the pigment-making proteins of melanoma.[^22][^19]" ([^19] Coulie et al. 2014 is the standard classification source and already does this work at line 226.)

**S5. HLA-C\*08:02 frequency is stated as a population range when it is two population-specific values (lines 376 and 305)**
- Quote (line 376): "HLA-C\*08:02, which she happened to inherit — as do only about 8–11% of Americans.[^24]" (Line 305 repeats "carried by only about 8–11% of Americans.")
- Problem: Tran 2016 states: "In the United States, the HLA-C\*08:02 allele is expressed in approximately **8% of whites and 11% of blacks**" (PMC5178827). "8–11% of Americans" reads as a measurement uncertainty rather than a difference between groups — and the figure's own data block (line 357) already has it right.
- Rewrite (both places): "…which she happened to inherit, as do roughly 8% of white and 11% of Black Americans." It is also worth one clause noting this is the share of people who carry the allele (each person has two HLA-C genes).

**S6. Viral antigens are oversold; the EBV parallel needs a caveat (line 230)**
- Quote: "The cancer depends on them, so it cannot easily stop making them, and they are thoroughly foreign. The Epstein–Barr virus plays a similar role in some lymphomas and in nasopharyngeal cancer.[^6] These are among the easiest tumor targets of all…"
- Problem: as written, a reader cannot explain why HPV-driven cancers exist at all in people with normal immune systems. Two real caveats are missing: (a) E6/E7 are expressed at low levels and HPV+ tumors routinely blunt presentation (HLA loss, low interferon signaling) — which is why they grow in immunocompetent people; (b) EBV is a poor parallel for "keeps making viral proteins": EBV-driven tumors use restricted latency programs — Burkitt lymphoma typically expresses only EBNA1, which is poorly presented on class I, while nasopharyngeal carcinoma adds LMP1/LMP2. "Plays a similar role" is fine for *causation*, misleading for *visibility*.
- Rewrite: "The cancer depends on them, so it cannot easily stop making them, and they are thoroughly foreign — which is why they are among the most attractive tumor targets we know. They are not free wins: these tumors arise in people with working immune systems, because the viral proteins are made in small amounts and the tumors learn to dim their displays (Chapter 7). The Epstein–Barr virus drives some lymphomas and nasopharyngeal cancer in a comparable way, though those tumors are craftier still: they switch off most viral proteins and keep only one or two, chosen partly because T cells see them badly.[^6]"

**S7. Cancer-testis antigens are described too absolutely (line 232; glossary line 723)**
- Quote: "A family of genes is normally switched on only in the developing sperm cells of the testis — and male germ cells carry no MHC class I, so they never display these proteins to T cells.[^20]"
- Problem: the germ-cell/MHC-I half is exactly right and well sourced ([^20] Gjerstorff 2015: "Male germ cells are devoid of HLA-class I molecules and cannot present antigens to T cells"), but the same review states the restriction is not absolute: "not all CT antigens are strictly limited to male germ cells in normal tissues. Some can be further classified as testis-restricted (only testis), testis-selective (testis and no more than 2 additional tissues) or testis/brain-restricted," and notes placental/trophoblast expression. The group is also >200 proteins in several families, not "a family". This matters downstream: it is why MAGE-A3-directed T cells killed two patients through cross-reactivity with MAGE-A12 in normal brain.
- Rewrite: "More than two hundred genes are switched on almost nowhere in a healthy adult except the developing sperm cells of the testis — and male germ cells carry no MHC class I, so they never display these proteins to T cells.[^20] (A few of them do turn up at low levels elsewhere, in the brain or placenta, which has cost patients their lives in trials of T cells aimed at them — Chapter 10.)" Soften the glossary entry likewise (see S13).

**S8. TESLA's feature list and the 98% filter are conflated (line 498)**
- Quote: "the features that best separated real neoantigens from duds were how tightly and how stably a fragment binds, how abundant it is in the tumor, how water-repelling it is, and where in it the mutation sits; combining them filtered out 98% of the non-immunogenic candidates.[^26]"
- Problem: in Wells 2020 the 98% filter comes from combining **four** features — binding affinity, tumor abundance, binding stability and (TCR) recognition/foreignness — and the paper reports the cost as well as the benefit: "filtering out 98% of non-immunogenic peptides while preserving 45% of immunogenic ones" (PMC7652061). Hydrophobicity and mutation position were separate findings, and the direction of the hydrophobicity effect is the opposite of what a reader will assume: immunogenic peptides were significantly **less** hydrophobic, and immunogenic peptides avoided mutations at anchor position 2 while being enriched at position 3.
- Rewrite: "the features that best separated real neoantigens from duds were how tightly and how stably a fragment binds, how abundant it is in the tumor, and how foreign it looks to the T-cell repertoire; combining those four filtered out 98% of the non-immunogenic candidates — while also throwing away more than half of the real ones. Two subtler patterns showed up too: the winners were slightly *less* water-repelling than the losers, and the change tended to sit just off the anchor positions.[^26]"

**S9. `ch06-antigen-kinds`: body-map zone count is wrong, and the HER2 card doesn't mention the organ the map tints (lines 285–288, 320–325)**
- Quote: "with 8 small organ zones that can be tinted (skin, eye, throat, thymus, lung, heart, breast, gut, cervix, testis…)" and, for HER2, "Found in healthy tissue: At low levels on many normal tissues, including lining cells of the lung and gut… Body map: tumor inset lit; breast, lung, gut, heart tinted lightly (low levels)."
- Problems: (a) ten zones are listed after "8"; a builder will guess. (b) The HER2 card tints the heart but never says why, and the heart is the one that matters clinically — HER2/ERBB2 is expressed in cardiomyocytes, which is the basis of trastuzumab cardiotoxicity (Loibl & Gianni, [^22]).
- Fix: say "ten small organ zones" (or list eight and drop two). In the HER2 card: "Found in healthy tissue: At low levels on many normal tissues — lining cells of the lung and gut, and heart muscle, which is why HER2 drugs are watched for heart effects (Chapter 9)."

**S10. `ch06-tmb`: mobile row arithmetic is wrong (lines 572–573)**
- Quote: "Consider collapsing to 22 rows by hiding (behind a 'Show all 29' button) Thyroid, Testicular, Prostate, Glioblastoma, Ovary, Liver, Esophagus, Osteosarcoma (21 rows remain)."
- Problem: 29 − 8 = 21, so "collapsing to 22 rows" contradicts "(21 rows remain)" in the same sentence.
- Fix: "Consider collapsing to 21 rows by hiding (behind a 'Show all 29' button) Thyroid, Testicular, Prostate, Glioblastoma, Ovary, Liver, Esophagus and Osteosarcoma."

**S11. The frameshift card promises more than frameshifts deliver (lines 459–462)**
- Quote: "'A frameshift' → passes all gates, with a flourish… 'A deleted letter shifts the reading frame, so everything downstream is a brand-new protein sequence. Many new fragments; a much better chance that one is seen.'"
- Problem: most frameshifts create a premature stop codon and the transcript is destroyed by nonsense-mediated decay, so the new protein is often never made — the reason indel neoantigens are *enriched* (ninefold for mutant-specific binding, Turajlic 2017, [^30]) rather than guaranteed. As written, the figure's one "flourish" card teaches a rule with no exceptions, in a figure whose entire thesis is that most typos fail.
- Fix: add a second clause to the card text: "…a much better chance that one is seen. (Often the cell spots the broken recipe and shreds the message before any protein is made — which is why frameshifts are the best bet, not a sure thing.)"

**S12. "KRAS is made in large amounts" conflates dependence with expression (line 425)**
- Quote: "Step 3 (made + cut): KRAS is made in large amounts (the cancer depends on it): gate 2 ✓."
- Problem: gate 2 asks whether the gene is switched on; KRAS is expressed at ordinary, not high, levels, and oncogenic dependence does not imply abundance. The step caption at line 486 gets it right ("so the cancer keeps making it").
- Fix: "Step 3 (made + cut): the mutant KRAS gene is switched on in every cancer cell, and the cancer cannot afford to switch it off: gate 2 ✓."

**S13. Clinic box: dMMR prevalence in colorectal cancer, and the decomposition that makes the next clause land (line 635)**
- Quote: "About 15–20% of them turn out to be deficient, most because the tumor has silenced one repair gene by itself; fewer than a third of the deficient tumors point to an inherited fault, Lynch syndrome…"
- Problem: the standard figure is **about 15%** of colorectal cancers overall — conventionally decomposed as ~12% sporadic (usually *MLH1* promoter hypermethylation) and ~3% Lynch syndrome; the 15–20% upper bound is not supported by the cited guideline ([^34] Sepulveda 2017). Giving the decomposition also *derives* the "fewer than a third" instead of asserting it (3/15 = 20%). Worth adding: in stage IV disease the share is only ~4–5%, which is why the checkpoint-inhibitor sentence that follows applies to a small slice of advanced patients. ([^27] Le 2017 supports the direction in its own Fig. 3 legend: MMR-deficient tumors were found "more often in early stage (defined as stage < IV) disease.")
- Rewrite: "About 15% of them turn out to be deficient: roughly 12% because the tumor silenced one repair gene by itself, and about 3% because of an inherited fault, Lynch syndrome, which can then be tested for in the patient's relatives.[^34] (In cancers that have already spread, the share is smaller — closer to one in twenty.) The same result now also flags patients whose advanced cancers are likely to respond to checkpoint inhibitors (Chapter 8)."

**S14. The eyelid study's method should be one clause longer (lines 9–11)**
- Quote: "sequenced the DNA of something utterly ordinary: 234 tiny snippets of normal eyelid skin, removed from four people aged 55 to 73 during routine eyelid surgery."
- Problem: they did not sequence "the DNA" — they sequenced "the coding exons of **74 genes** implicated in skin and other cancers" to great depth (PMC4471149). This matters for the number the paragraph then quotes: ~140 driver mutations per cm² counts only drivers in those 74 genes, so it is a floor, not a total. (Everything else is exact: 234 biopsies; four donors, three female and one male, aged 55–73; blepharoplasty; "a patchwork of thousands of evolving clones"; "over a quarter of cells".)
- Rewrite: "…read 74 cancer genes, letter by letter, in 234 tiny snippets of normal eyelid skin removed from four people aged 55 to 73 during routine eyelid surgery." And at line 11: "about 140 such mutations in every square centimeter of skin — and that is counting only the 74 genes they looked at.[^1]"

**S15. Two glossary entries (lines 723, 728)**
- Quote: "cancer-testis-antigen | Cancer-testis antigen | A protein normally made only by developing sperm cells, which display nothing on MHC class I, but switched back on in many cancers."
- Problem: "which display nothing on MHC class I" dangles (it reads as if the protein, not the sperm cell, does the displaying) and is absolute; see S7 on testis-selective members.
- Rewrite: "A protein made almost nowhere in a healthy adult except developing sperm cells — which carry no MHC class I and so never show it to T cells — but switched back on in many cancers. Examples include NY-ESO-1 and MAGE-A4."
- Quote: "tumor-mutational-burden | Tumor mutational burden (TMB) | The number of mutations in a tumor's DNA, usually counted per million DNA letters (megabase)."
- Problem: TMB is counted per megabase of the *sequenced* region — in practice the protein-coding part, which is why exome-based and panel-based numbers differ (Chalmers 2017 is cited in the chapter precisely on this point).
- Rewrite: "…usually counted per million letters of the DNA that was actually read — normally the protein-coding part. Numbers from different tests are not directly comparable."
