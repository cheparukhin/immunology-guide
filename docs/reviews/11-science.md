# Chapter 11 "Teaching the Body to See": scientific review

Reviewed: `content/drafts/11-vaccines.md` (549 lines; line numbers below refer to that file). Reviewed against `docs/PLAN.md` §2 (voice rules, "no hype", company-reported labeling) and §3 (Ch 11 scope). Verification date: 2026-10-05. Sources checked against PubMed, FDA.gov, ClinicalTrials.gov, and company press rooms. Note: the Amass subscription hit its usage limit partway through (resets 2026-11-03), so regulatory and trial checks were completed via FDA.gov, ClinicalTrials.gov API and PubMed instead.

## Overall assessment

This is the most carefully sourced draft I have reviewed in this project, and on the numbers it is remarkably clean: all 29 sources exist, every volume/page/DOI I checked matches exactly, and the HPV figure's data block reproduces Falcaro, Lei and Palmer essentially verbatim down to the confidence intervals — including the admirable instruction not to invent an interval for Scotland's zero. The KEYNOTE-942 numbers (157 patients, 22% vs 40%, HR 0.56, CI 0.31–1.02, p=0.053, 5-year HR 0.51 and 0.17–1.35 for OS), the MAGRIT details (13,849 screened, a third positive, 13 injections over 27 months, HR 1.02), sipuleucel-T (512 men, 21.7→25.8 months, no PFS effect), Kenter, Ott, Sahin, Rojas, Sethna, Pant, Andtbacka, Chesney, Todo and the RP1 label are all accurate, and the chapter's handling of company-reported topline data for INTerpath-001 and the BioNTech colorectal stop is a model of the PLAN's labeling rule. The problems are concentrated in a few places: one flatly incorrect historical claim about Taiwan's vaccination program, one absolute claim ("no personalized neoantigen vaccine had been approved anywhere") that a November 2025 Russian authorization makes false, a delivery-technology error that merges two genuinely different mRNA platforms, two statements about oncolytic viruses that are more confident than the evidence (one of which contradicts the chapter's own argument), and — most consequential for a reader — a figure caption that uses the TESLA 6% figure to describe vaccine-induced responses when TESLA measured something else entirely. Seven must-fix, sixteen should-fix.

## Must fix

**1. Taiwan did not vaccinate "every newborn" from July 1984.**
- Location: l.104. Quote: "In July 1984, Taiwan began vaccinating every newborn with a single harmless protein from the virus's surface."
- Problem: from July 1984 to June 1986, the free program covered only infants born to HBsAg-positive (high-risk) mothers. Coverage was extended to all newborns in July 1986. This is well documented and is why much of the HBV literature keeps two dates in play (several papers index cohorts to "the July 1986 HBV vaccination program"). The error matters twice over: it is the chapter's single historical date for the "first anticancer vaccine," and the draft's own next sentence leans on maternal infectivity, which is exactly the distinction the 1984–86 phase was built around.
- Evidence: Chang MH et al., *Gastroenterology* 2016;151:472-480.e1 (PMID 27269245, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1053/j.gastro.2016.05.048)) — the cited source itself dates cohorts from the program start; program phasing confirmed in the Taiwan HBV program literature, e.g. Hsu/Chen, "Risk of HBV infection among male and female first-time blood donors born before and after the July 1986 HBV vaccination program in Taiwan," *BMC Public Health* 2021 ([PMC8502303](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC8502303/)).
- Suggested rewrite: "In July 1984, Taiwan became the first country to launch a national hepatitis B immunization program, vaccinating the newborns at highest risk — those born to infected mothers — with a single harmless protein from the virus's surface. From July 1986 every newborn was included."

**2. "No personalized neoantigen vaccine had been approved anywhere" is no longer true.**
- Locations: l.324 and takeaway l.462 ("no such vaccine is approved").
- Problem: on 21 November 2025 Russia's Ministry of Health authorized clinical use of NeoOncovac, a personalized anti-tumor mRNA neoantigen vaccine from the Gamaleya Center with the National Medical Research Radiological Centre and the Blokhin Center, for adults with inoperable or metastatic cutaneous melanoma (in combination with immunotherapy) and after resection of metastatic lesions. The first patient was treated in April 2026. No randomized efficacy data have been published. The chapter's point — that the evidence base does not yet support approval in the US or EU — is right, but "anywhere" is an absolute claim and it is wrong, which is precisely the kind of statement a skeptical reader will check.
- Evidence: FSBI "NMMRC" of the Russian Ministry of Health, [announcement](https://new.nmicr.ru/en/news/for-the-first-time-in-the-world-approval-has-been-granted-for-the-use-of-a-personalized-mrna-vaccine/) (21 Nov 2025); first administration reported April 2026 ([gxpnews](https://gxpnews.net/en/2026/04/russia-administers-personalized-cancer-vaccine-neooncovac-for-the-first-time/)).
- Suggested rewrite (l.324): "…overall survival was still being followed, and no personalized neoantigen vaccine had yet been approved by the FDA or the European Medicines Agency. (Russia's health ministry authorized clinical use of a personalized mRNA melanoma vaccine, NeoOncovac, in November 2025; no randomized results have been published.)" Takeaway: "…survival data are pending, and no such vaccine is yet approved in the US or Europe."

**3. The figure's "6% trigger T cells" caption misdescribes what the TESLA study measured, and understates vaccine immunogenicity by several-fold.**
- Location: figure `ch11-personal-vaccine`, step 3 verbatim caption, l.280. Quote: "In real tests of top-ranked predictions, only a small minority trigger T cells: about 6% in one large consortium study. That is why real vaccines carry up to 20 or 34 targets, not six."
- Problem: TESLA did not vaccinate anyone. It took top-ranked predicted neoepitopes from six patients (3 melanoma, 3 NSCLC) and tested whether *pre-existing* T cells in matched PBMC or TIL bound them by pMHC-multimer assay; 37 of 608 (6%) did. That is a spontaneous-recognition rate in unvaccinated patients, not the fraction of vaccine targets that a vaccine elicits a response against. The actual vaccine numbers from the chapter's own sources are much higher: Ott 2017 — vaccine-induced T cells targeted 58 of 97 unique neoantigens (60%) for CD4 and 15 of 97 (16%) for CD8; Rojas 2023 — 25 of 230 administered neoantigens (11%) by ELISpot across all patients, and 25 of 106 (24%) in responders. A reader who takes the caption literally concludes that 94% of a real vaccine's beads are inert, which is both wrong and more pessimistic than the chapter's own evidence section. (The main-text version at l.236, "only 37, about 6%, were recognized by T cells," is accurate as written, and the deep-dive's 98%-filtering claim at l.309 is accurate too — the problem is only this caption's "trigger.")
- Evidence: Wells DK et al., *Cell* 2020;183:818-834.e13 (PMID 33038342, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cell.2020.09.015)) — "608 peptides selected from among the top-ranked peptides from all groups… were tested for immunogenicity by pMHC multimer-based assays and 37 (6%) of those were found to be immunogenic," with reactivity detected "in subject-matched peripheral blood mononuclear cells (PBMCs) and/or tumor infiltrating T lymphocytes (TILs)"; Ott PA et al., *Nature* 2017;547:217-221 (PMID 28678778, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/nature22991)); Rojas LA et al., *Nature* 2023;618:144-150 (PMID 37165196, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06063-y)).
- Suggested rewrite: "Predictions are imperfect in both directions. When one consortium tested 608 top-ranked predicted fragments for T cells that patients already had, only 6% scored. In real vaccine trials more targets land — in one melanoma study, 60% of the chosen fragments drew a helper-T-cell response and 16% a killer response — but which ones is hard to call in advance. That is why real vaccines carry up to 20 or 34 targets, not six."

**4. Autogene cevumeran is an RNA-lipoplex given intravenously, not a lipid nanoparticle injection; the chapter merges two different platforms.**
- Locations: l.231 (step 5), l.232 (step 6), and glossary `lipid-nanoparticle` (l.501) as applied to both drugs.
- Quote: "The chosen stretches… are strung together like beads in one strand of mRNA and wrapped in {{lipid-nanoparticle|lipid nanoparticles}}, as in the COVID-19 mRNA vaccines.[^15]" and "**Inject.** Doses are given over months."
- Problem: [^15] is Rojas 2023, which describes autogene cevumeran as "an individualized neoantigen vaccine based on uridine mRNA-lipoplex nanoparticles" — an RNA-liposome (RNA-LPX) complex given by intravenous infusion, designed to reach splenic antigen-presenting cells. Intismeran autogene is a lipid-nanoparticle product given intramuscularly. Attributing LNP-and-injection to both, with "as in the COVID-19 mRNA vaccines" attached to the pancreatic citation, is wrong about the one drug the sentence cites. The related claim at l.313 — "a practical design choice for a single mRNA molecule" — is also inaccurate for autogene cevumeran, whose vaccine comprised two RNA strands, each encoding up to 10 MHC class I and class II neoepitopes.
- Evidence: Rojas LA et al., *Nature* 2023;618:144-150 ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06063-y)); Khattak A et al., *J Clin Oncol* 2026;44:2482-2489 (PMID 42223134, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO-26-00835)) — "nine doses of intramuscular intismeran 1 mg."
- Suggested rewrite (step 5): "**Wrap.** The chosen stretches, each a short run of protein around one mutation, are strung together like beads on mRNA and wrapped in fat. The two leading vaccines package it differently: intismeran autogene uses lipid nanoparticles, like the COVID-19 mRNA vaccines, and is injected into a muscle; autogene cevumeran uses an RNA–fat complex delivered into a vein, aimed at the immune cells of the spleen." Step 6 becomes "**Give.** Doses are given over months, usually alongside a checkpoint inhibitor."

**5. Japan's approval of teserpaturev is conditional and time-limited, not a plain approval — and the chapter flags exactly this for RP1 two sentences later.**
- Location: l.358. Quote: "Japan approved teserpaturev (G47Δ; Delytact) for malignant glioma, a brain cancer, in 2021, after a small single-arm trial in which 16 of 19 patients with recurrent or residual glioblastoma were alive a year later."
- Problem: the June 2021 authorization was a *conditional and time-limited* marketing approval under Japan's regenerative-medical-product pathway, with continued approval contingent on verifying clinical benefit in a post-marketing comparative study within seven years. The draft describes RP1's accelerated approval honestly ("further trials must confirm the benefit") but gives teserpaturev — approved on a 19-patient single-arm trial — no such caveat, which inverts the strength of the two evidence bases. The takeaway at l.463 then lists "T-VEC, teserpaturev and RP1" together as "approved examples" with no indication that two of the three are provisional.
- Evidence: PMDA Report on the Deliberation Results, 24 May 2021 ([pmda.go.jp](https://www.pmda.go.jp/files/000242808.pdf)); Daiichi Sankyo launch release, 1 Nov 2021 ([PDF](https://www.daiichisankyo.com/files/news/pressrelease/pdf/202111/20211101_E.pdf)); Todo T et al., *Nat Med* 2022;28:1630-1639 (PMID 35864254, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-022-01897-x)).
- Suggested rewrite: "Japan gave teserpaturev (G47Δ; Delytact) a conditional, time-limited approval for malignant glioma, a brain cancer, in 2021, on the strength of a single-arm trial in which 16 of 19 patients with recurrent or residual glioblastoma were alive a year later; a comparative study must confirm the benefit for the approval to stand." Takeaway: "…approved examples such as T-VEC, teserpaturev and RP1 help a minority of patients, and two of the three were cleared provisionally, pending confirmatory trials."

**6. "It reaches only tumors a needle can reach" is wrong, and it contradicts the chapter's own in-situ-vaccination argument and its own figure.**
- Location: l.356. Quote: "But it reaches only tumors a needle can reach, and adding it to pembrolizumab did not improve survival in a 692-patient trial."
- Problem: the whole point of the preceding three paragraphs, the `ch11-oncolytic` figure, and quiz Q4 is that T-VEC works partly through a systemic T-cell response that reaches uninjected tumors. The OPTiM lesion-level analysis showed exactly that: tumor area fell by ≥50% in 64% of injected lesions, 34% of uninjected non-visceral lesions and 15% of visceral lesions, with complete resolution in 47%, 22% and 9% respectively. The real limitation is different and more interesting: the distant effect is modest and inconsistent, and the drug's indication and benefit are confined to injectable disease. As written, the sentence tells the reader the chapter's central mechanism does not happen. Relatedly, Chesney 2023 missed *both* dual primary endpoints (PFS HR 0.86, p=0.13; OS HR 0.96, p=0.74), so "did not improve survival" is incomplete.
- Evidence: Andtbacka RHI et al., "Patterns of Clinical Response with Talimogene Laherparepvec (T-VEC)…," *Ann Surg Oncol* 2016;23:4169-4177 (PMID 27342831, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1245/s10434-016-5286-0)) — "T-VEC resulted in a decrease in size by ≥50% in 64% of injected lesions (N = 2116), 34% of uninjected non-visceral lesions (N = 981), and 15% of visceral lesions (N = 177)"; Chesney JA et al., *J Clin Oncol* 2023;41:528-540 (PMID 35998300, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.22.00343)).
- Suggested rewrite: "The systemic effect is real but modest: in the same trial, injected deposits shrank by at least half in 64% of cases, uninjected skin and lymph-node deposits in 34%, and tumors in internal organs in only 15%. T-VEC is still given only where a needle can reach, and adding it to pembrolizumab improved neither progression-free nor overall survival in a 692-patient trial.[^27]" (Add [^26] / the 2016 lesion-level paper as a new source.)

**7. The ELI-002 phase 2 result is an unpublished company topline and is not labeled as such.**
- Location: l.338. Quote: "But a randomized phase 2 trial in 144 people with resected pancreatic cancer, reported in June 2026, missed its main goal of delaying recurrence. Subgroups that seemed to benefit are a hypothesis for a new trial, not a result.[^24]"
- Problem: PLAN §2 requires that "results known only from a company press release or topline announcement (not yet peer-reviewed) must be labeled as such." The chapter does this scrupulously for INTerpath-001 ("This is company-reported topline news") and for the BioNTech colorectal stop ("According to the company… the data are unpublished"), but not here, even though [^24] is an Elicio press release and the ITT hazard ratio and p value were not disclosed. The second sentence shows good judgment about the subgroups; it just needs the provenance attached.
- Evidence: Elicio Therapeutics press release, 15 June 2026 ([globenewswire](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/06/15/3311657/0/en/elicio-therapeutics-reports-results-from-phase-2-amplify-7p-study-and-outlines-refined-phase-3-development-strategy-for-eli-002-7p-in-adjuvant-pancreatic-cancer.html)) — "AMPLIFY-7P did not meet the pre-specified primary endpoint of disease-free survival ('DFS') in the intent-to-treat population"; post-hoc R0 subgroup HR 0.65, p=0.048, n=121, median DFS 23.8 vs 12.8 months; T-cell-response correlation HR 0.22, p<0.0001, n=90.
- Suggested rewrite: "But in June 2026 the company reported that a randomized phase 2 trial in 144 people with resected pancreatic cancer had missed its main goal of delaying recurrence; it did not release the overall numbers. Subgroups that seemed to benefit — and they were defined after the fact — are a hypothesis for a new trial, not a result.[^24]"

## Should fix

**8. The four-fixes figure assigns MAGRIT to "shared normal protein / the thymus deleted the best-fitting T cells," which the chapter's own deep-dive says is not what happened.**
- Locations: figure `ch11-four-fixes` preset B (l.188) and micro-caption (l.177); deep-dive l.214.
- The deep-dive is explicit and correct: "MAGE-A3 is a *cancer-testis antigen*… Tolerance to such proteins was expected to be weaker than to ordinary self proteins. So MAGRIT's failure shows that tolerance is not the whole story." But preset B sets switch 1 to "Shared normal protein," which fires the caption "The thymus deleted the T cells that fit this protein best. The few that are left bind loosely." A reader who plays the figure and then opens the box gets contradictory explanations of the same trial.
- Fix: give preset B its own caption override, e.g. "MAGE-A3 is made mainly in the testis, so tolerance to it should have been weak — yet 2,312 people vaccinated after lung-cancer surgery with a potent adjuvant relapsed just as often as on placebo. Shared tumor proteins fail for more reasons than tolerance alone." Alternatively relabel switch 1 as "Shared tumor protein" | "Tumor mutations (neoantigens)" and soften the micro-caption to "T cells that fit shared proteins the body already makes are rarer and usually bind more loosely."

**9. Preset B says MAGRIT "vaccinated 2,312 people"; 2,312 were randomized and 1,515 received the vaccine.**
- Location: l.188. Vansteenkiste 2016: "2312 patients met all eligibility criteria and were randomly assigned to treatment: 1515 received MAGE-A3 and 757 received placebo and 40 were randomly assigned but never started treatment." The main text at l.122 handles this correctly ("got a vaccine against MAGE-A3… or a placebo"); only the figure caption is wrong.
- Evidence: PMID 27132212, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/S1470-2045(16)00099-1).
- Fix: "The MAGRIT trial randomized 2,312 people after lung-cancer surgery to the shared tumor protein MAGE-A3 with a potent adjuvant, or to placebo."

**10. The takeaway attributes Sweden's 88% to vaccination "at age 12–13"; Lei's 88% is for vaccination before age 17.**
- Location: l.459. Quote: "HPV vaccination at age 12–13 has cut cervical cancer among young women by about 87–88% in England and Sweden."
- Lei reports a fully adjusted IRR of 0.12 (95% CI 0.00–0.34) for women vaccinated **before 17**, not for 12–13 specifically; the Swedish study did not resolve a 12–13 band. The figure's own data block gets this right ("Vaccinated before age 17"), so only the takeaway over-reaches.
- Evidence: PMID 32997908, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1056/NEJMoa1917338).
- Fix: "Early HPV vaccination has cut cervical cancer among young women sharply: by 87% in England for those offered it at 12–13, by 88% in Sweden for those vaccinated before 17, with no cases recorded in Scotland among those vaccinated at 12 or 13."

**11. "Each vaccine was made in six weeks or less" will be read as "vaccinated six weeks after surgery"; the median time to the first dose was 9.4 weeks.**
- Location: l.231. Rojas 2023 set benchmarks of "produce vaccines in ≤6 weeks" and "administer first dose of the vaccine in ≤9 weeks," and reports "median time to autogene cevumeran was 9.4 weeks (range of 7.4–11.0 weeks)," having hit the benchmarks "within 1 and 3 days of respective benchmarked times." Six weeks is the manufacturing step; the clinically relevant interval is about nine weeks.
- Evidence: PMID 37165196, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-023-06063-y).
- Fix: "In one study, manufacturing took about six weeks, and patients had their first dose a median of nine weeks after surgery."

**12. ELI-002 2P and 7P are treated as one product, and the phase 2 did not select for residual disease the way the phase 1 did.**
- Locations: l.338 (and the AMPLIFY-201 sentence at l.338 immediately before it).
- AMPLIFY-201 used ELI-002 **2P** (G12D and G12R peptides) in 25 patients selected for minimal residual mKRAS disease by ctDNA and/or serum tumor antigen. AMPLIFY-7P used ELI-002 **7P** (seven peptides) in patients with resected stage I–III mKRAS-driven PDAC who were radiographically disease-free, with no MRD requirement, versus observation. Calling both "ELI-002" and placing them in consecutive sentences implies the phase 2 simply scaled up the phase 1, when the shift in both the product and the eligible population is one of the main reasons the results diverge — and it is the kind of detail that makes the "subgroups are a hypothesis" sentence land.
- Evidence: Pant S et al., *Nat Med* 2024;30:531-542 (PMID 38195752, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41591-023-02760-3)); Elicio press release, 15 June 2026.
- Fix: name the versions ("ELI-002 2P," then "a seven-peptide version, ELI-002 7P") and add one clause: "this time in patients who were disease-free on scans, whether or not molecular traces of cancer could be found."

**13. The Taiwan reduction is the crude rate ratio; the age-adjusted estimates are 58–74% and shrink with age.**
- Locations: l.104 and takeaway l.459 ("roughly three-quarters").
- Chang 2016's crude rates (0.23 vs 0.92 per 100,000 person-years) do give ~75%, and the draft quotes them correctly. But the paper's Poisson relative risks by age band were 0.26 (6–9 years), 0.34 (10–14), 0.37 (15–19) and 0.42 (20–26) — a 58% reduction in the oldest band. Since the chapter is otherwise scrupulous about saying that early vaccine data may overstate lifetime effects (l.98 does this for HPV), the same hedge belongs here, and the age gradient is itself informative.
- Evidence: PMID 27269245, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1053/j.gastro.2016.05.048).
- Fix: "…about three-quarters lower in the vaccinated birth cohorts than in those born before the program: 0.23 versus 0.92 cases per 100,000 people per year. The gap narrows with age — from about 74% in children to 58% in the 20-to-26-year-olds — so, as with HPV, the lifetime effect will take decades to pin down."

**14. "Most of the remaining cases" overstates what Chang 2016 reports.**
- Location: l.104 (repeated l.117). The paper's conclusion is associational: "Transmission of HBV from highly infectious mothers and incomplete immunization were associated with development of HCC." It does not quantify these as a majority of residual cases.
- Fix: "The cases that still occurred clustered among children who had missed doses or whose mothers carried very high levels of the virus."

**15. The oncolytic simulation's default distant-tumor effect is the best case, not the typical one.**
- Location: figure `ch11-oncolytic`, phase 5 (l.392) and final note (l.398). The default run ends with the uninjected tumor cut from 12 cells to 5 — a 58% reduction — labeled "the distant effect is real but partial." The real distribution: ≥50% shrinkage in 34% of uninjected non-visceral lesions and 15% of visceral lesions, and a durable response rate of 16.3% overall. The verbatim final note ("the effect is real but inconsistent… many patients do not respond at all") is good and should stay, but the animation is the thing readers will remember, and it currently shows a strong systemic response as the default outcome.
- Fix: reduce the distant-tumor end state to roughly 9 of 12 cells remaining (and keep the injected tumor's partial clearance), and put a number in the note: "In the trial behind T-VEC's approval, injected deposits shrank by at least half in about two thirds of cases, uninjected skin and lymph-node deposits in about a third, and tumors in internal organs in about one in seven. Many patients do not respond at all."
- Evidence: Andtbacka 2016 *Ann Surg Oncol* (PMID 27342831); Andtbacka RHI et al., *J Clin Oncol* 2015;33:2780-2788 (PMID 26014293, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1200/JCO.2014.58.3377)).

**16. The personal-vaccine game's step 4 implies that every cell carrying a recognized target dies.**
- Location: figure `ch11-personal-vaccine`, step 4 (l.281) and step caption (l.296): "T cells destroy every cancer cell that carries a target they recognize."
- The coverage logic is exactly right and worth teaching, but as implemented the model has no escape other than clonal heterogeneity: recognized cells are cleared completely. The chapter's own narrative says otherwise — in KEYNOTE-942, 22% of vaccinated patients still relapsed; in Rojas, half the patients mounted no response and two responders recurred; and Sahin's relapse came from B2M loss in cells that *were* targeted. Showing complete clearance of every targeted clone risks teaching that neoantigen coverage is sufficient.
- Fix: in step 4, leave a few outlined-but-surviving cells inside the cleared patches and add one verbatim line: "Even cells with a recognized target are not all killed — some stop displaying it, and some sit where T cells cannot work. This shows why coverage matters, not that coverage cures."

**17. The four-fixes outcome strip reads as an absolute result, but KEYNOTE-942's comparison was vaccine-plus-pembrolizumab versus pembrolizumab alone.**
- Locations: outcome tiers (l.166-169) and preset C caption (l.189).
- With brakes "Released" and a shared-protein target the figure returns tier 1, "An immune response, but little or no clinical benefit" — which, read plainly, says anti-PD-1 in microscopic disease achieves little. Adjuvant pembrolizumab on its own is standard of care precisely because it reduces relapse; the vaccine's contribution in KEYNOTE-942 and INTerpath-001 is an increment on top of it, since both arms received pembrolizumab.
- Fix: retitle the third indicator "Outcome (compared with anti-PD-1 alone)" and adjust tier wording: tier 0 "No added effect on the tumor"; tier 1 "An immune response, but no clear added benefit"; tier 2 "The combination behind recent trial successes: fewer relapses than anti-PD-1 alone." Add one line to the disclaimer: "Anti-PD-1 after surgery already lowers the risk of relapse; what the vaccine adds is what trials measure."

**18. The opening's "early-to-mid twenties" does not describe the whole Scottish cohort.**
- Locations: l.9 and l.13. Palmer's cohort was women born 1 January 1988 to 5 June 1996, with data extracted in July 2020 — so the group spanned roughly 24 to 32 years of age, not "early-to-mid twenties." The sentence is true of the women vaccinated at 12–13 (the youngest cohorts, vaccinated from 2008), which is the group that matters for the zero, but as written the antecedent is "those women," i.e. everyone just described.
- Evidence: PMID 38247547, [DOI](https://doi.org/10.1093/jnci/djad263).
- Fix: "But the women vaccinated at 12 or 13 were only in their mid-twenties when the data were pulled in 2020, and cervical cancer becomes more common later in life."
- The figure's dataset note ("when the youngest were in their mid-twenties," l.72) is fine and can stay.

**19. Morales 1976 cannot support "It remains a standard treatment."**
- Location: l.203, citation [^13] at the start of the sentence. The 1976 report is the right source for the origin; it says nothing about 2026 practice. Either add a current source (e.g. a current EAU/AUA non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer guideline, or Kamat et al.'s BCG reviews) or soften to "and it is still in use half a century later." Note also that "Since 1976, doctors have treated…" compresses history: the 1976 paper was the first report; BCG was not FDA-approved for bladder cancer until 1990.

**20. The BioNTech colorectal trial's comparator and prior futility signal are both worth one clause each.**
- Location: l.332. Quote: "…stopped a randomized phase 2 trial that gave autogene cevumeran alone, without a checkpoint inhibitor, after colorectal-cancer surgery to patients whose blood still carried tumor DNA."
- The company's statement specifies that the comparator arm was "watchful waiting, the current standard of care" — worth saying, because "randomized… gave autogene cevumeran alone" can read as single-arm, and because a no-treatment control is what makes the overall-survival imbalance notable. The statement also records that "the futility boundary was crossed in October 2025," ten months before termination, with the board judging the data then too immature. Both facts strengthen the chapter's honesty without adding length.
- Evidence: BioNTech SE statement, 28 Aug 2026 ([biontech.com](https://www.biontech.com/int/en/home/mediaroom/news/statements/2026/08/BioNTech-Provides-Update-on-Phase-2-Clinical-Trial-of-Autogene-Cevumeran-in-Resected-Colorectal-Cancer.html)).
- Fix: "…to patients whose blood still carried tumor DNA, comparing it with watchful waiting, the standard of care in that situation. The trial had already crossed a futility boundary in October 2025. According to the company, independent monitors then judged…"

**21. Source [^22]'s URL is the wrong regional path.**
- Location: l.541. The draft gives `biontech.com/us/en/home/mediaroom/…`; the statement resolves at `biontech.com/int/en/home/mediaroom/news/statements/2026/08/BioNTech-Provides-Update-on-Phase-2-Clinical-Trial-of-Autogene-Cevumeran-in-Resected-Colorectal-Cancer.html`. **22. "About 1,100" understates INTerpath-001's enrollment; the companies reported 1,137 randomized.**
- Location: l.324. The registry's planned enrollment was ~1,089 (and the 2024 design abstract says "approximately 1089"), but the August 2026 release states 1,137 patients randomized 2:1. Since the draft is reporting the readout, use the readout's number.
- Evidence: Merck/Moderna release, 19 Aug 2026 ([moderna](https://news.modernatx.com/merck-and-moderna-announce-phase-3-interpath-001-trial-of-intismeran-plus-keytruda-met-endpoints-of-rfs-and-dmfs-in-melanoma)); NCT05933577.

**23. The HLA toggle's notice ("New patient, new predictions") contradicts the figure's own micro-caption and quiz Q3.**
