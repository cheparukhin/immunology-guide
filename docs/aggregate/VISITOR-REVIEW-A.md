# Visitor review A: first-time reader + editorial design critic

**Journey:** Home → Ch 1–5 at desktop (1440, light). Home, Ch 2 and Ch 4 on a phone (390). Ch 3 in dark theme. Ch 8 for consistency.
**Method:** I clicked through every figure step, preset and sandbox on Ch 1–5 and timed the autoplay modes. I hovered glossary terms and citations and answered quizzes. Evidence is in the session scratchpad under `visitorA/` (contact sheets `sheet-*.png`). There were no console errors, failed requests or overflow on any page I loaded.

---

## Overall verdict

This is a beautiful, serious, trustworthy book. The home hero and the first two Chapter 1 figures earn a Kurzgesagt fan's attention within a minute. The typography is calm and bookish, and the honesty badges ("ILLUSTRATIVE", "demo odds vs real odds") build real credibility. Part I is strongest where a figure has **one idea and one control**: the zoom, the sickle-cell sandbox, inflammation, the macrophage dial, antibody jobs, the kill.

It weakens where a figure grows into a dashboard. Chapter 3's clonal-selection figure and most of Chapter 5 put a scene, a magnifier, a chart and a badge legend into one stage, with 9–10 px labels. In these figures the caption also ends up far below the Next button. Autoplay is too slow: one "Build another" replays a full 60-second tour.

Fix the dense figures, the pacing and the end-of-chapter wayfinding, and this goes from very good to exceptional.

---

## What's genuinely excellent (don't break it)

- **Home page as onboarding.** The animated hero with its legend, "The big idea in 30 seconds" (3 steps), and "Built in layers", which demos the drop cap, a figure, a Go-deeper box, a glossary term and a source inline. This is the best "how to read this site" I've seen.
- **Reading typography.** The serif body sits at a comfortable ~68-character measure with generous leading, drop caps, quiet section rules and a thin-numeral chapter mark. The "Key idea" pull-outs and the "In the clinic" callouts are distinct but not loud. Dark theme is fully coherent (Ch 3 checked).
- **Glossary and citation popovers.** Definition, "Introduced in Chapter N" and a Glossary link, plus citation previews with the DOI. The desktop side TOC hides itself when a wide figure overlaps it. The active-section highlight works.
- **Fig 1.1 Seven powers of ten.** It goes from hand to fingerprint, skin, capillary, T cell, virus, antibody and finally the atom-bead contact. It reads at a glance, and the ending is a real "oh". This sets the bar.
- **Fig 1.2 sandbox.** The "sickle-cell change" turns the protein into a fiber and the red cell into a sickle. "A stop" truncates to 5 beads. This is perfect cause and effect.
- **Fig 2.2 inflammation.** The four sign chips (Redness, Heat, Swelling, Pain) light up as each mechanism appears. **Fig 2.4** has a single dial plus a "Place it in a tumor" toggle. Both are model "one idea" figures.
- **Fig 3.4 One molecule, four jobs.** Tabs plus "Without / With antibodies" is the clearest before/after on the site. **Fig 5.2 Anatomy of a kill** has a nanometer magnifier, a "≤ 80 s pores repaired" clock and a clean 8-step story.
- **Fig 4.4 lymph-node search.** The "Now the real odds" calculator (1 / 10 / 100 dendritic cells → 33 h / 3 h / 20 min) is exactly the kind of honesty-plus-insight that explorables fans love.
- **Phone adaptations.** Stages re-flow to portrait, the 4.2 matrix moves below the stage, the 2.1 tray becomes tiles, and the drawer menu shows sub-sections with a "HERE" badge. There is no horizontal overflow.
- **Consistency into Part III.** Ch 8 uses the same shell and hero lens. Data figures are light cards; mechanism figures are dark stages. That grammar holds.

## Per-page notes

**Home.** Strong pull: the dark hero, the cell legend and the "No biology background needed · About 5 hours" line. "The big idea in 30 seconds" (3 steps) lands the thesis. "Built in layers" is a smart onboarding. The grid has orphans (#15). The interlude card with its 1891–2024 timeline is charming.

**Ch 1 · A Crash Course in Cells.** This is the best chapter for first impressions. 1.1 zoom and 1.2 recipe sandbox are both A+. 1.3 binding gets the idea across (snug, look-alike, wrong shape, with an occupancy count) but has two control rows and an empty readout area. 1.4 census is clean, honest and nicely toggled (By place / type / weight). The prose before 1.1 is redundant (#9). The glossary and citation popovers are delightful.

**Ch 2 · The First Responders.** 2.1 tray is fun ("What if it dies messily?" is a great beat). However, the 8 numbered sensors are keyed to a legend below the stage, so the eye ping-pongs between them. Consider labeling the lit sensors in place. 2.2 is excellent but its Play all is far too slow (#3). In 2.3 the balance-scale metaphor works, but the stage is busy with "hollow: everyday activating signals" and two sliders plus four presets. 2.4 is excellent. The complement section wants a picture (#9).

**Ch 3 · A Library of Infinite Keys.** The writing is the best in Part I (the Landsteiner opener). 3.1 is a great concept with a broken sandbox loop (#1). 3.2 ladder is cramped (#13). 3.3 is overloaded and mislabeled (#2). 3.4 is excellent. Citation punctuation is inconsistent (#11). Dark theme looks great here.

**Ch 4 · How T Cells See.** This is the conceptual heart, and the shop-window / evidence-board metaphors carry it well. 4.1 is clear step by step (scenarios need captions, #6). 4.2 is the hardest graphic (#7). 4.3 cross-presentation gets its idea across with its switch, but early steps leave dead space. 4.4 is a highlight thanks to the real-odds calculator. 4.5 has the order, gating and caption problems (#4, #6). There are seven Go-deeper boxes, some stacked.

**Ch 5 · Killers, Helpers and Brakes.** 5.2 kill is gorgeous. 5.1 thymus works, and the "Run the thymus" tally is nice, but the single thymocyte is hard to track among similar beige cells (give it a halo). 5.3 and 5.4 are the densest figures on the site (#5). By here, the cumulative cognitive load is high. This is the chapter that most needs simpler figures.

**Phone (home, Ch 2, Ch 4).** Overall a very good responsive job. The menu drawer works well, and tap-to-define popovers are well placed. The main problems are caption distance in control-heavy figures (#4) and blank reserved gaps (#14).

**Dark theme (Ch 3).** Fully consistent: stages, callouts, the light-card chart (3.2) and popovers all read well. Nothing broke.

**Part III sample (Ch 8).** Same shell, typography, hero lens and quiz and takeaways pattern. The survival-curve and side-effects body map are light data cards, consistent with 1.4 and 3.2. The figures look calmer than Ch 5, so the density problem is local to a few Part I figures rather than systemic.
