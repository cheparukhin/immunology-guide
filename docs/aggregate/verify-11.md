## 1. Errors (MUST fix)

### E1. T-VEC was not the first oncolytic virus approved in Europe
**Location:** L340.
> "The first oncolytic virus approved in the United States and Europe, in 2015, was **talimogene laherparepvec (T-VEC; Imlygic)**…"

**What's wrong:** T-VEC was the first FDA approval (27 Oct 2015) and the first **EU-wide, centrally authorized** oncolytic virus (Imlygic, 16 Dec 2015). But a national European approval came eleven years earlier: Latvia registered **Rigvir** (an unmodified ECHO-7 enterovirus) for skin melanoma in **2004**; the registration was withdrawn in 2019 after the State Agency of Medicines found vials containing far less virus than claimed. A reader who checks "first in Europe" will find Rigvir immediately, and the chapter's credibility on 2026 regulatory claims rests on exactly this kind of precision.
**Evidence:** Amass EMA record `AMRC_S5WvI5BLl5gaE9DTBNUK52aU2w2` (Imlygic, centralised human procedure, first authorization 2015-12-16); Alberts P et al., "The advent of oncolytic virotherapy in oncology: The Rigvir story", *Eur J Pharmacol* 2018;837:117–126 (Latvian registration 2004); Latvian State Agency of Medicines actions, March 2019.
**Fix:** "The first oncolytic virus licensed by the FDA and by the European Medicines Agency, in 2015, was…" (an optional clause — "an earlier, unmodified virus had been registered in Latvia in 2004 and later withdrawn" — would also serve the chapter's honesty about weak evidence).

### E2. MAGRIT: 2,312 were randomized, not treated
**Location:** L124.
> "In MAGRIT, one of the largest randomized trials, 2,312 people whose lung cancers had been removed received a placebo or a vaccine against MAGE-A3…"

**What's wrong:** 2,312 met eligibility and were randomly assigned; **1,515 received the vaccine, 757 received placebo, and 40 never started treatment**. The chapter's own deep-dive (L199) states this correctly, so the narrative contradicts the box two pages later. (This is the figure-caption error the first review flagged as should-fix 9, now migrated into the main text.)
**Evidence:** Vansteenkiste JF et al. *Lancet Oncol* 2016;17:822–835, PMID 27132212 — "2312 patients met all eligibility criteria and were randomly assigned to treatment: 1515 received MAGE-A3 and 757 received placebo and 40 were randomly assigned but never started treatment".
**Fix:** "…2,312 people whose lung cancers had been removed were randomly assigned to a placebo or a vaccine against MAGE-A3…"

### E3. The pancreatic-recurrence claim attributes the clone analysis to the wrong patients
**Location:** L322.
> "in the two responders whose cancer returned, the tumors had lost the cancer-cell lineages the vaccine targeted: evolution under police pressure, caught in the act.[^19]"

**What's wrong:** two responders did recur (patients 25 and 14), but the exome/phylogenetic analysis showing loss of clones carrying immunogenic vaccine neoantigens was performed in **patient 14 and patient 29** — and patient 29's solitary liver lesion explicitly did not meet the criteria for clinical recurrence. Patient 25, the other recurrent responder (who died of local recurrence), is characterized by *weak vaccine immunity*, not by sequenced clone loss. The paper's abstract-level statement ("recurrent PDACs were pruned of vaccine-targeted cancer clones") is what the chapter should track.
**Evidence:** Sethna Z et al. *Nature* 2025;639:1042–1051, PMID 39972124 (PMC11946889): the two recurrences are patients 25 and 14; the clone-fraction comparison of primary vs recurrent tumours is reported for patients 14 and 29, the latter a "disappearing solitary liver micrometastasis (criteria for clinical recurrence not met)".
**Fix:** "and in the recurrent tumors that were sequenced, the cancer-cell lineages carrying the vaccine's targets had been pruned away: evolution under police pressure, caught in the act.[^19]"

## 2. Unsupported or weakly supported (SHOULD fix)

### U1. BCG: a current-practice claim with no source anywhere in the chapter
**Location:** L192. The first review's item 19 was resolved by deleting the Morales 1976 citation, which leaves "For about half a century, doctors have also treated early bladder cancer by washing BCG… into the bladder" and the scorecard row "BCG (bladder) | Standard; not tumor-specific" with no evidence at all; the interlude carries the history, not today's standard of care. Add a current guideline (AUA/SUO or EAU non-muscle-invasive bladder cancer guideline) or Kamat AM et al. *Nat Rev Urol* 2017 for BCG's continuing role, and note that FDA licensure for bladder cancer came in 1990.

### U2. IMCODE003 cannot isolate the vaccine's contribution
**Location:** L324 — "A randomized trial of 260 patients, IMCODE003, is under way." Accurate (NCT05968326, 260 participants, randomized, open-label, active/not recruiting), but the comparison is **autogene cevumeran + atezolizumab + mFOLFIRINOX versus mFOLFIRINOX alone**: a positive result will not separate the vaccine from the checkpoint inhibitor, which matters because the preceding paragraph's caution is precisely about attribution. One clause: "…though it tests the vaccine plus a checkpoint inhibitor against chemotherapy alone, so a win would not tell us which of the two did the work."
**Evidence:** ClinicalTrials.gov NCT05968326 (arms: "Autogene Cevumeran + Atezolizumab + mFOLFIRINOX" vs "mFOLFIRINOX"; allocation randomized, masking none).

### U3. T-VEC's lesion percentages are lesion-level, not patient-level
**Location:** L340 ("Injected deposits shrank by at least half in 64% of cases") and the `ch11-oncolytic` final note ("in about two thirds of cases"). The denominators are **lesions**: 64% of 2,116 injected lesions, 34% of 981 uninjected non-visceral lesions, 15% of 177 visceral lesions, in the 295 patients randomized to T-VEC. "Cases" will read as patients. Suggest "of injected deposits" / "of uninjected deposits in skin and lymph nodes".
**Evidence:** Andtbacka RHI et al. *Ann Surg Oncol* 2016;23:4169–4177, PMID 27342831.

### U4. RP1's 24% is measured in a subset, and the approval history is a strong honesty beat the chapter omits
**Location:** L342. "In its single-arm trial, tumors shrank in 24% of evaluable patients" is exact (ORR 24.2%, 95% CI 15.8–34.3; median duration of response 14.1 months) — but "evaluable" means the **91 of 140 enrolled patients who had at least one non-injected lesion**, which is itself the distant-effect question the chapter cares about. Worth one clause. Also notable, and consistent with the chapter's no-hype rule: the accelerated approval followed two FDA complete response letters (July 2025, April 2026) and a 10–3 advisory-committee vote.
**Evidence:** FDA, 6 Aug 2026 — https://www.fda.gov/drugs/resources-information-approved-drugs/fda-grants-accelerated-approval-vusolimogene-oderparepvec-wtpg-combination-nivolumab-melanoma (IGNYTE, NCT03767348).

### U5. NeoOncovac's authorization is broader than "for advanced melanoma"
**Location:** L310. The Russian release authorizes use "in adult patients with inoperable or metastatic cutaneous melanoma — in combination with immunotherapy. Or as a standalone therapy after surgical treatment of cutaneous melanoma, when all metastatic lesions have been removed". "For advanced melanoma" is not wrong but drops the adjuvant arm of the authorization, which is the setting the rest of the section is about.
**Evidence:** FSBI NMRRC release, 21 Nov 2025 — https://new.nmicr.ru/en/news/for-the-first-time-in-the-world-approval-has-been-granted-for-the-use-of-a-personalized-mrna-vaccine/

### U6. Pre-existing anti-herpes immunity, not just vaccine-induced antibody
**Location:** deep-dive L403 — "antibodies, including those raised by earlier doses, can neutralize it". Most adults are already HSV-1 seropositive, so for a herpes-based agent pre-existing immunity is usually the dominant constraint; the clause as written implies the problem is self-inflicted by repeat dosing. (Flagged as optional in the first review; still open.) Suggest: "antibodies against the virus — many patients already carry them from past cold sores, and more are raised by each dose — can neutralize it."

### U7. "several are in development for precancers" (therapeutic HPV vaccines, L103)
Uncited and unquantified in a chapter that otherwise sources its pipeline claims; true (E6/E7-directed candidates in CIN2/3 and vulvar precancer). Either cite one (e.g. Kenter GG et al. *N Engl J Med* 2009;361:1838–1847, the long-peptide VIN3 study the earlier draft used) or soften to "a few".

## 3. Citation problems

- **C1. [^23] (Chesney 2023) is cited for T-VEC's identity, engineering and 2015 approval (L340).** Chesney is the 692-patient pembrolizumab-combination phase 3; it supports only the second use of [^23] in that paragraph. The descriptive sentence belongs to [^22] (Kaufman review) and/or [^24].
- **C2. The headline efficacy figure "16% … versus 2%" has no matching source.** The durable-response rate (16.3% vs 2.1%) is from the OPTiM primary report, **Andtbacka RHI et al. *J Clin Oncol* 2015;33:2780–2788 (PMID 26014293)**, which is not in the source list; [^24] (Ann Surg Oncol 2016) reports only the lesion-level 64/34/15% figures and the progression-prior-to-response analysis. Add the 2015 paper.
- **C3. The INTerpath-001 design claim rests on the registry, not on the cited releases.** "two to one, to the vaccine or a placebo, both alongside pembrolizumab" is confirmed by NCT05933577 (arms "Intismeran autogene + Pembrolizumab" vs "Placebo + Pembrolizumab", triple-masked); the Merck/Moderna release gives 1,137 randomized 2:1 and the endpoints met but describes the control as pembrolizumab alone (one secondary summary even states no placebo was used). [^2] already lists the NCT number — cite it explicitly for the design so the discrepancy cannot be mistaken for an error in the draft.
- **C4. [^4] (WHO position paper) is cited for "many countries vaccinate boys too" (L41).** The position paper recommends gender-neutral programmes where feasible; it is not a tally of national programmes. Either attribute the recommendation ("WHO supports vaccinating boys where feasible") or cite a coverage source.
- **C5. [^22] (Kaufman 2015) carries the present-tense pipeline claim** "Dozens of other platforms are in trials, built from adenoviruses, poxviruses, reoviruses and more" (L401) — an eleven-year-old review for a 2026 statement. Add a recent review or write "have been built".
- **C6. [^13] (Rojas 2023) is doing eight jobs**, including the "up to 20 neoantigens" cap, the RNA-lipoplex platform, the 9-week first dose, the 11% ELISpot rate, the COVID-vaccine control, the class-I/II design and the 18-of-19 manufacturing success. All are genuinely in that paper (verified in PMC10171177), so this is a readability rather than an integrity problem — but the single call site at L216/L217 now supports two different products' design choices, and a reader cannot tell which clause [^13] versus [^14] covers. Consider splitting the citation across the two sentences.
