## 1. Errors (MUST fix)

### E1. The dostarlimab/rectal-cancer status sentence is now out of date in one respect
**Location:** clinic box, L295.
> "A larger trial, AZUR-1 (154 patients, no comparison group), has met its main goal according to its sponsor, and US regulators are due to decide on approving dostarlimab for this use by February 2027.[^17]"

**What's wrong:** the facts as given are right (AZUR-1, 154 patients, single-arm, primary endpoint of sustained clinical complete response at 12 months met, PDUFA date February 2027), but the application was granted **priority review** and qualifies for the FDA's national priority voucher programme, so the decision can come earlier than the stated date — and the chapter presents February 2027 as the decision point without that caveat, in a box a patient or family may act on. The earlier review flagged the same passage (S6); the revision took the guideline half of the fix (now correct and verified) but not the "possibly sooner" half.
**Evidence:** GSK press release, 24 Aug 2026 — https://www.gsk.com/en-gb/media/press-releases/jemperli-dostarlimab-accepted-for-priority-review-by-the-us-fda/ (priority review; PDUFA February 2027; "not currently approved anywhere in the world for locally advanced rectal cancer"); PCORI horizon scan entry for AZUR-1.
**Fix:** "…and US regulators are due to decide by February 2027, possibly sooner under a priority-review voucher."

*(Nothing else in the chapter contradicted a primary source. The two 2026-dated regulatory statements that most needed re-checking — the May 2026 ctDNA approval and the April 2025 China approval of ivonescimab for the HARMONi-2 population — are both exactly right, and the latter avoids the 2024/2025 confusion the first review caught.)*

## 2. Unsupported or weakly supported (SHOULD fix)

### U1. "In 2025 the World Health Organization added pembrolizumab to its Model List of Essential Medicines" (L497)
Literally supported for the three new indications, but it reads as a first-ever listing. Pembrolizumab has been on the EML since **2019**, where nivolumab is listed for metastatic melanoma with the square-box note "pembrolizumab is an alternative". What 2025 added were the cervical, colorectal and lung indications (with atezolizumab and cemiplimab as lung alternatives).
**Evidence:** 21st WHO Model List of Essential Medicines (2019), antineoplastics section: "nivolumab* … Metastatic melanoma / * pembrolizumab is an alternative"; PAHO/WHO release, 8 Sep 2025 — "Pembrolizumab was added to the EML as a first-line monotherapy for metastatic cervical cancer, metastatic colorectal cancer, and metastatic non-small cell lung cancer."
**Fix:** "In 2025 the World Health Organization extended pembrolizumab's place on its Model List of Essential Medicines to advanced cervical, colorectal and lung cancers (it had been listed for melanoma since 2019)." Also note the source says *metastatic*, not "advanced".

### U2. Three standard-of-care claims in the "cold tumors" paragraph carry no citation (L158)
"chemotherapy plus a PD-1 blocker is a standard first treatment for many lung cancers", "an anti-PD-L1 antibody after chemoradiation is standard for some stage III lung cancers", and the abscopal-effect characterization ("rare enough that single cases still get written up") are all uncited; the earlier draft at least cited Formenti for the radiation combination. The PACIFIC evidence is quotable and strengthens the paragraph: 5-year overall survival 42.9% with durvalumab vs 33.4% with placebo after chemoradiation.
**Evidence:** Spigel DR et al. *J Clin Oncol* 2022;40:1301–1311, PMID 35108059.

### U3. "similar approaches are now approved for some lung and head and neck cancers" (L290) is correct but unsourced
Verified against the current Keytruda label (Amass FDA record `AMRC_MxKexSDwyxBFvU9MDP0ydS66Tyh`), which carries both the perioperative resectable-NSCLC indication (KEYNOTE-671) and "resectable locally advanced HNSCC whose tumors express PD-L1 (CPS ≥1)" (KEYNOTE-689). One citation — or a pointer to the FDA label — would make the chapter's only unsourced approval claim traceable.

### U4. Ivonescimab: the US regulatory state is missing from an otherwise balanced box (L486)
The deep-dive correctly says the global picture is weaker and cites the published HARMONi trial (PFS 6.8 vs 4.4 months, HR 0.52; overall survival 16.8 vs 14.0 months, HR 0.79, 95% CI 0.62–1.01 — i.e. not significant). What a reader will ask next is whether it is approved outside China: the FDA accepted a BLA with a target action date of **14 November 2026**, and an interim analysis of the global HARMONi-3 squamous cohort did not cross its significance threshold in 2026. One clause each.
**Evidence:** Le X et al. *Lancet Oncol* 2026;27:1094–1109, PMID 42636833; Summit Therapeutics releases (BLA acceptance; 12 Aug 2026 NMPA squamous approval, which also dates the first Chinese approval to May 2024).

### U5. "Most computer-predicted neoantigens turn out to be ignored by T cells" (L400)
True and owned by Chapter 11, but this chapter states it without a source while making a strong claim about AI's limits in the same sentence. A cross-reference exists; consider also carrying Chapter 11's evidence pointer so the sentence is auditable on its own.

### U6. One figure caption still slightly outruns its evidence
`ch12-ctdna` step 5: "Patients with tumor DNA in their blood lived longer, on average, with immunotherapy, while most whose tests stayed negative did well without it." Both halves are right (median OS 32.8 vs 21.1 months; 88% disease-free at two years among 357 persistently negative patients), but the second half invites the inference that the test *identified* people who did not need treatment, which IMvigor011 did not test — the narrative says so explicitly (L398) and the caption does not. Worth one clause, since the figure is the memorable artifact.
**Evidence:** Powles T et al. *N Engl J Med* 2025;393:2395–2408, PMID 41124204.

## 3. Citation problems

- **C1. [^26] (ASCO Post) is a news item standing in for a regulatory document.** It supports the facts (approval 15 May 2026; MIBC after cystectomy with ctDNA molecular residual disease; Signatera CDx companion diagnostic; based on IMvigor011) and the entry sensibly points to the Tecentriq label, but the primary record is now verifiable directly: the FDA label carries the indication "adjuvant treatment of adult patients with muscle invasive bladder cancer (MIBC) after cystectomy who have circulating tumor DNA molecular residual disease" (Amass FDA record `AMRC_YAINHCBh0h4iw28o5WlzZ4aka8C`). Cite the label or the FDA approval notice as the primary and keep the news item as secondary.
- **C2. [^16] cites the *patient* version of the NCCN guideline for a professional-practice claim.** The claim itself is verified — the 2026 patient guideline (derived from NCCN Rectal Cancer v2.2026, 7 Apr 2026) states that for locally advanced dMMR/MSI-H disease the preferred primary treatment is six months of immunotherapy — but a statement about what "US treatment guidelines" recommend should cite the clinician guideline (NCCN Clinical Practice Guidelines in Oncology, Rectal Cancer, v2.2026), with the patient version as the open-access pointer.
- **C3. [^29] (ecancer) carries two different kinds of fact.** It does support both the WCLC 2026 interim overall-survival numbers (30.8 vs 22.6 months; HR 0.73, 95% CI 0.57–0.95) and the April 2025 Chinese approval for this population — so the first review's [^40] problem is fixed — but a news report is now the chapter's only source for a national regulatory action. Add a company/NMPA release (e.g. Summit, 12 Aug 2026) so the approval does not rest on journalism.
- **C4. The pathologic-response definitions are attributed to [^13] (NADINA).** NADINA applies the International Neoadjuvant Melanoma Consortium categories; the defining paper is Tetzlaff MT et al., *Ann Oncol* 2018;29:1861–1868. Acceptable as is (NADINA states the thresholds), but if the deep-dive is meant to teach the definitions, cite their source.
- **C5. Two claims inside figure specs have no traceable source in the chapter.** `ch12-ctdna`'s builder note ("ctDNA has preceded imaging by a median of about three months in bladder cancer and by several months in colorectal cancer") is correct — median 96 days in muscle-invasive bladder cancer (Christensen E et al. *J Clin Oncol* 2019;37:1547–1557, PMID 31059311) and a mean of 8.7 months in colorectal cancer (Reinert T et al. *JAMA Oncol* 2019;5:1124–1131, PMID 31070691) — but neither paper is in the source list. Since the note is explicitly "for the builder's orientation only", a bracketed reference in the spec would be enough.
